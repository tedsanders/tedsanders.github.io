# On AI safety

## Power is dangerous

Power is the ability to cause outcomes. Danger is the ability to cause harmful outcomes. It's hard to be powerful without being dangerous.

The more powerful you expect AI to get, the more seriously you should take AI danger.

If an AI burns down your house, you can recover. If an AI burns down your planet, you cannot.

## Harm can be classified by intent

AI can cause harm in 3 ways:

- The harm is intended by a human directing the AI

- The harm is intended by the AI itself

- The harm is accidental

### Human-intended harm

To prevent harm intended by humans, we seek:

- Ways to limit harmful intent

- Ways to limit harmful ability

- Ways to apply these limits to humans across the world

Today, we prevent human harm by:

- Threatening force against wrongdoers

- Incentivizing cooperation via trade

- Limiting access to high leverage technologies (e.g., nukes)

AI threatens this equilibrium if:

- AI provides greater leverage

- AI reduces trade and interdependency

- AI proliferation is hard to limit

### AI-intended harm

To prevent harm intended by AI, we need:

- A way to limit harmful intent OR a way to limit harmful ability

- A way to apply this to all AI in the world

Today:

- AI alignment science tries to limit harmful intent

- AI control tries limit harmful ability

- Both require human alignment to be applied to AI

### Accidental harm

To prevent accidental harm, we need:

- To foresee hard-to-foresee consequences

- To be robust against unforeseen consequences

## Proliferation

To do harm, AI can:

- Produce outputs that do harm (e.g., weapons, cyberweapons, bioweapons)

- Produce AIs that do harm

Today, AI companies seem to suppress directly harmful outputs like computer viruses more than they try to suppress indirectly harmful outputs like machine learning code.

If AI becomes powerful, we may control AI inputs (e.g., chips, code, data) more than AI outputs, akin to how we control uranium enrichment technology more than enriched uranium itself.

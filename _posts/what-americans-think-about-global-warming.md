# What Americans think about global warming

39% of Americans say that global warming will probably cause humans to go extinct.[^1] 
![](/assets/images/quw_Image_1.png)

In accordance with that belief, Americans show concern to Gallup?s underspecified questions:

- 46% of Americans worry a great deal about global warming

- 45% of Americans think global warming will pose a serious threat in their lifetime

- 38% of Americans think the news underestimates the seriousness of global warming

- 60% of Americans say the environment should have priority over the economy[^2] 
And yet:

In Washington state, where about twice as many voters identified as Democrat vs Republican in 2018:[^3] 
- 59% of voters rejected a revenue-neutral carbon tax that lowered sales tax by 1%[^4] 
- 56% of voters rejected a carbon tax that funded projects to reduce pollution[^5] 
Most Americans don?t even know that the science is pretty one-sided:

- 32% of Americans don?t know that most scientists believe in global warming[^6] (progress has been slow - this was 39% 20 years ago)

- 87% of Americans don?t know that more than 90% of climate scientists believe in global warming[^7]

## Notes

[^1]:  By ?probably cause humans to go extinct? I mean that they selected a number 50% or higher on a slider in a half-hour online survey. Uncertainty was +-3%. N was 1,266. Year was 2017. Sampling looks solid - they sampled by phone number & zip code, and if people didn?t have computers with which to answer, they provided them. My biggest worry is that by asking respondents to answer via slider (with a text box backup), they may have biased the answers upward. However, from a brief Google search, slider bias research seems mixed. Roster et al. found for 1-5 ratings sliders made very little difference. Thomas et al. found that for payments sliders biased people toward the endpoint, which I interpret as acting as a second anchor in anchoring bias. Buskirk summarizes a number of results, one of which is that sliders that default to middle or right positions are less likely to be moved, but that requiring the slider be moved induces an overcorrection bias, where right sliders are moved down and left & middle sliders are moved up.
[^2]:  Gallup, March 2020
[^3]:  In Washington state, voters don?t register by party. But when Crosscut asked which party they would register for if they had to, about 40% say Democrats and about 20% say Republicans, though it moves year to year.
[^4]:  Washington Initiative 732 was rejected by voters in 2016.
[^5]:  Washington Initiative 1631 was rejected by voters in 2018.
[^6]:  https://news.gallup.com/poll/308876/environmental-ratings-global-warming-concern-flat-2020.aspx
[^7]:  https://climatecommunication.yale.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Climate-Change-American-Mind-May-2017.pdf
